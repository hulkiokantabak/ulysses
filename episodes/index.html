By episode — where the day was read

EPISODE TITLES ARE SCHEMA SCAFFOLDING, NOT JOYCE'S — HE PRINTED NONE (ANCHOR A2).

This is not an episode companion. It is an index of the project's own work, set down where it falls in the day's text: the objects tracked through an episode, the women who enter there, the instruments of rule that mark it, the places where one first reading actually struggled. No entry summarises an episode. None carries reading guidance. Everything is derived from the dimensions and verified against the 1922 text at every build; empty is shown as empty.

Each section carries four more things now. The book's own words, wherever the record warrants them. The day that stretch was read, and the page of the record where the exchange starts. The hours and places base document 03 documents in that episode. And the one place the episode turns away from Homer.

A passage is shown only where something in the record warrants it, and exactly two things count. An exchange title from the study's own table of the sixteen exchanges, every row of which carries a page you can open. Or the reader's own words, where the study marks them as his and records the page he said them on — the record's own voice, and the stronger of the two. Each passage prints its warrant in full, so you can see how much it actually names rather than take this project's word for it. The choice of lines is always the project's.

What does not count is this project's own prose about the reading — its episode-by-episode rows, its own address lines — however carefully those were made. Nor do the answers in the record, which are also this project's. That boundary has been drawn wrong twice and corrected twice; the corrections are catches C-53 and C-57.

Two more rules worth knowing before you read down. An exchange is placed in an episode only when the exchange's own title names that episode — no inference, which leaves three exchanges unplaced and listed below rather than assigned. And where nothing warrants a passage, the section says so and says what it searched: the record's shape is not a verdict on the episode, and a missing warrant is not a missing reading.
