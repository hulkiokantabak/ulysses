The advisors and their sessions

Two panels govern this project, and their jurisdictions do not touch. The literary masters judge reading — which dimension deserves attention. The nine advisors judge building — whether the artifact works, whether the link resolves, whether a first reader can use it. The chair proposes; the author ratifies.

They are never merged. Corrected 28 July 2026. They have now sat together twice — on 27 July and again on 28 July — and the joint sessions found things neither body had found alone. The standing rule when they meet is attack across the aisle: every opening must name and break a member of the other body, and a colleague does not count. The jurisdictions still do not touch, which is why the format works; what was wrong was the word never.

  • The nine advisors — the build panel: two technologists, three literary advisors, two historians, two chair's picks. Its routing table and type discipline are there too.
  • The masters session — the adversarial session on which dimension to build next. It produced ten of them, and the finding that reorganised them all.
  • Panel session 01 — do we go to Code now? Unanimously yes, with four binding vetoes. One of those the author later overruled, and the record of that stands in the log.
  • Panel session 02 — the design of the site. Three rounds, seven advisors, four directions on the ballot, two killed by argued veto. The outcome is applied in the design record.
  • Joint session 09 — the two bodies together, on what this project has built read through the book, through Homer, and through the newest Odyssey. Seventeen voices across three durations, and an adversarial verifier printed unedited at the end, including where it is wrong about this project. It found a passage the project had been arguing from and never showing.

The other session records — 03 through 08 — are working documents and stay in the repository rather than on this site. The line is not seniority: this one was written to be read by somebody who was not in the room, and those were written to settle a build. Where a working session produced a ruling that binds, the ruling is on the surface it binds, and the reasoning is in the log.
