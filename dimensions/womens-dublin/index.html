The Women's Dublin

Dimension 2 · Type I, Inventory (extract a category, enumerate, tabulate) · Build status: built · Panel score: 7

Chair's pick of the Type-I three (anchor A11). Built to every register it budgets: HTML, presentation, and — 19 July 2026 — the document PDF. The diagram is skipped by budget. Amended 4 August 2026, chair's resolution on 'women-drawn-never-named' (data/author-items.json, under the recorded delegation of 2026-08-04): dimension 2 now also carries a separately denominated register, §drawn-never-named in women.html and women.print.html — the women the 1922 text draws and never names. Its admission rule is stated in the artifact; its passages are machine-verified; it is UNCOUNTED by design — no total on any surface, because a count would assert the reachability the sixteen have and the register exists to mark its absence (L-38). Its rows are partitioned out of the census tally by the section's data-denominated="uncounted" marker; the census's 181 is unchanged.

The claim
Collected as they actually enter the 1922 text, the women of Dublin appear far more often at one remove — a letter, a photograph, an arm, the subject of men's talk — than on stage; and the text's grant of an inner life runs almost opposite to a woman's independence.
The unit
the woman (~15) — income, mobility, interiority; and how she enters the text
What it shows
Of sixteen women, Molly alone is given a sustained inside — and she is the least mobile, forty pages without leaving the bed. Milly, the only young woman with a stated wage, and Mrs Riordan, the rentier with her own means, reach the reader almost entirely as paper and others' recollection; Mrs Dignam and Ann Hathaway exist as men's arithmetic and men's theory. How a woman enters the text is the dimension's real subject — a fact about the book, not a flaw in the map (learning L-02, from catch C-02).

The rule that built it

The inclusion rule
"This dimension collects the women as they actually enter the 1922 text — how each earns or is kept, where each can and cannot go, and whether the text grants her an inside or only a surface." Boundary: "Excluded as tropes, not persons (the C-02 boundary)". Stated in artifacts/womens-dublin/women.html (the tropes-not-persons boundary in the What-was-left-out section).
What was rejected under it
Kept: the "What was left out" section, artifacts/womens-dublin/women.html (the What-was-left-out section, after the register) — (1) near-misses cut for overlap: Miss Dunne, Zoe Higgins, Mrs M'Guinness and Mrs M. E. White, Nurse Callan, the applewoman; (2) routed to Dimension 3, not lost: Queen Victoria, Lady Dudley/the vicereine, Mary Kelly, the courted slavey; (3) excluded as tropes, not persons: Kathleen ni Houlihan/Granuaile/Old Gummy Granny, the sea as "great sweet mother", the Blessed Virgin, Eve/Helen/Penelope and Deasy's catalogue; (4) Katey, Boody and Maggy Dedalus folded into Dilly's entry.
What kind of thing it claims
A REPORTED DISTRIBUTION — under joint session 09's discriminating test (an inventory can only be attacked at its inclusion rule; a reading at its claim): Its claim field (data/dimensions.json) states a frequency over its own census — women "appear far more often at one remove... than on stage" — plus an anti-correlation observed inside that census ("runs almost opposite"). Session 09 grouped dim 2 with the inventories attacked only at the frame (docs/panel-session-09.md); Ginzburg's sharpest challenge asked it be restated "as an observation about four women" (same file) — an attack on the qualifier and the admission rule, not a counter-instance. The artifact says of itself: "An inventory is a reference, not an argument" (women.html).
