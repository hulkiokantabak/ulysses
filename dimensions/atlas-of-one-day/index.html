The Atlas of One Day

Dimension 14 · Type VI, Made object (a work, not a document about a work) · Build status: planned · Panel score: not yet scored

This dimension is planned. It has not been built. The sections below are empty because nothing has been made yet, not because something is hidden.

Rescoped by the author, 19 July 2026: built from publicly available material only, with a credits page carrying every source, licence and URL — and if a rights problem could not be avoided, the dimension was to be struck. It can be avoided, so it stands (catch C-32). **The caption-free form turns out to be what makes the licensing lawful, not what threatens it**: CC BY 4.0 permits attribution to live in a colophon rather than beside the image, and CC BY-SA 2.0's clause requiring credit 'at least as prominent as' any other authorship credit is satisfied vacuously when no panel carries any credit at all. Warburg's refusal of the caption and the licence compliance are the same decision — which also means that adding one tasteful signature to a panel would break every share-alike licence on it at once. Dropped as uncleanable: the newspaper page (no free source exists for a 16 June 1904 Dublin page image, and no substitute for it is recorded in the source manifest), Joyce's handwriting and any manuscript image (the Rosenbach grants no permission, and unpublished Joyce stays in copyright in the UK until 2039), photographic portraits of Joyce, and the separate tram map (Thom's 1904 carries the tram information already). Both rights gates cleared 19 July 2026 (C-33), and the author reopened the dimension on 25 July 2026 (C-48): the work is sourcing, batch by layer, and no panel is assembled before the slots are filled. Corrected 25 July 2026, two claims: this note said 'the Evening Telegraph masthead on Commons substitutes' for the dropped newspaper page, and no such file appears in the source manifest, in the fetched provenance, or anywhere else in this repository — an asserted substitute nobody had recorded; and it said 'the panels themselves are the next session's work', which C-48 superseded (anchor A4). The design's refusal of imagery — under which this dimension was proceeding, a contradiction flagged and left for the author — was reversed on 19 July 2026 (C-35). The dimension is now consistent with the design it is built under. Still the strongest candidate for the project's eventual physical form.

Sources

  • Wikimedia Commons, per file and per licence — the place layer. Period photographs come from the Commons re-hosts of the Lawrence Collection, restricted to files attributed to Robert French (d. 24 June 1917, so public domain in Ireland and the EU since 1 January 1988); contemporary photographs from files under CC0, CC BY or CC BY-SA, with Irish freedom of panorama (CRRA 2000 s.93) clearing the architecture underneath.
  • Thom's Official Directory of Ireland, 1904 — the 61st annual publication, the volume Joyce himself worked from — digitised at 600dpi by the University of Victoria. Public domain by age; the holding institution asserts no licence, only a 'No Known Copyright' statement, which is a statement of its knowledge and not a warranty. The clearance rests on the age of a 1904 commercial compilation, and the credits page says so as reasoning rather than citing it as a licence.
  • Ordnance Survey six-inch sheets of Dublin — Crown copyright expired. Sourced from the National Library of Scotland directly. NOT from Tailte Éireann, whose terms require a copyright permit for its own scans, and NOT from the Digital Repository of Ireland, which applies its own CC BY-NC-ND reuse licence and links back to NLS anyway. The licence was read on 19 July 2026 (catch C-33) rather than described: the per-image tag on the NLS Dublin sheets reads 'Re-use: CC-BY (NLS)', which the terms page resolves to CC BY 4.0 — not NC, not ND, not SA — with the required credit 'Reproduced with the permission of the National Library of Scotland' and a link to maps.nls.uk for online use. County Dublin is held in a revision surveyed 1906–1909 and printed 1909–1912, which is the layer this project wants. Corrected 25 July 2026. For six days after C-33 this entry read, verbatim: 'To be sourced from the National Library of Scotland's series via the Digital Repository of Ireland, NOT from Tailte Éireann, whose terms require a copyright permit for its own scans. The governing licence line is UNREAD and is the dimension's first gate.' Both halves were false by then — DRI is the one door clause 7 forbids, and the licence had been read — while the confidence field on this same page recorded it read and the gate closed. The withdrawn sentence is quoted here in full rather than described, because a correction that paraphrases the thing it withdraws leaves a reader unable to check it (anchor A4, learning L-27).
  • The 1922 text of Ulysses — public domain in Ireland and the EU since 1 January 2012, and in the United States.

Confidence

A rights survey of four material classes, each conclusion adversarially challenged, was run on 19 July 2026 (catch C-32) after the author rescoped the dimension to publicly available material. Its load-bearing finding: **an institution's terms are not the copyright.** The National Library of Ireland and Tailte Éireann both assert re-use conditions over their own scans, but neither holds copyright in a French photograph or a Victorian Ordnance Survey sheet. The remedy is therefore sourcing, not permission — the same image is taken from a repository that imposes no such terms. Every image will carry a named licence at a named URL on the credits page, and nothing enters the dimension without one. **Both gates were cleared on 19 July 2026 (catch C-33) and the dimension is buildable at three registers.** For the record, and in the past tense they belong in, they were: (1) The object layer — the third of the panel's three layers, which are not the same thing as the dimension's output registers — had not been surveyed at all; no item had been cleared, and if eighteen objects could not each be given a licence, the panel would have fallen from three layers to two. (2) The Ordnance Survey licence line had not been read by anyone: a peer-reviewed paper recorded the sheets being supplied to the Digital Repository of Ireland under CC-BY, but the repository returned 403 and the National Library of Scotland's terms page returned 405, so this rests on a description of the arrangement rather than on the arrangement itself. Both are now closed. The object layer cleared 17 of 18, each with a named licence at a named URL; the one that did not clear (episode 17) is ruled to take a period document in its slot, which is still a second layer — a ruling, not an acquisition, since the manifest records a candidate and a preference among three and no file has been fetched. Clearance is not acquisition, and the two were apart until 25 July 2026, when the seven cleared-but-unfetched objects (episodes 4, 7, 8, 10, 13, 15, 16) were acquired: the object layer now holds 17 files for its 17 cleared slots, each carrying the clearance-comparison's recorded verdict. The Ordnance Survey licence was read rather than described: the per-image tag on the NLS Dublin sheets is CC-BY, resolving to CC BY 4.0 — not NC, not ND, not SA — with a required credit line and a link. Two doors that looked right were not: the Digital Repository of Ireland applies its own CC BY-NC-ND reuse licence and points back at NLS anyway, and Wikimedia Commons holds no OS Ireland Dublin sheets at all. One build constraint follows from the source: the NLS image server caps a request at about half a megapixel, so there is no whole-sheet download and the panels must tile.
